The big ideas

1 · Scaled copies & scale factor

A scaled copy is a copy of a figure where every side length in the original is multiplied by the scale factor.

copy length = scale factor × original length
scale factor = copy lengthoriginal length
  • Angles stay the same. Only lengths change.
  • Perimeter gets multiplied by the scale factor too.
  • Area gets multiplied by scale factor × scale factor.
  • Adding the same amount to every side does not make a scaled copy. You must multiply.
  • Changing only one direction (only taller, only wider) is not a scaled copy.
  • Direction matters: big → small has a scale factor less than 1 (like 12); small → big is more than 1.

2 · Scale drawings

A scale is a ratio of drawing distance to actual distance.

1 inch : 50 feet → 1 inch on the drawing = 50 feet in real life
  • Drawing → actual: multiply. 4 inches × 50 = 200 feet.
  • Actual → drawing: divide. 300 feet ÷ 50 = 6 inches.
  • A scale without units (like 1 to 20) works with any unit, as long as both sides use the same unit: 1 cm to 20 cm, 1 in to 20 in…
  • Watch the units! Scale 1 to 72 with an 8-inch model: 8 × 72 = 576 inches = 576 ÷ 12 = 48 feet.
  • Which map is bigger? The one where 1 inch stands for less real distance (1 in : 1,000 ft is bigger than 1 in : 5,000 ft).

3 · Dilations

A dilation is a transformation that can change the size of a figure but not its shape (angles stay the same). Every dilation has a center point and a scale factor.

  • Each image point is on the ray from the center through the original point, scale factor × as far from the center.
  • Scale factor less than 1 → smaller; equal to 1 → the same; more than 1 → bigger.
  • The center doesn't move. If the center is a vertex, that vertex stays put.
  • Image points get a prime mark: A → A′ ("A prime").
Center at origin: (x, y) → (kx, ky)

Any center: count how far the point is from the center (right/left, up/down), multiply both by k, and count that far from the center again.

Find the center: draw lines through A and A′, B and B′. They cross at the center.
Find k: image distance ÷ original distance.

4 · Similar figures

Two figures are similar if there is a sequence of translations (slides), rotations (turns), reflections (flips), and dilations that takes one to the other.

  • Similar figures have equal angles and sides in the same ratio.
  • Congruent figures are similar with scale factor 1: same shape and same size (only slides, turns, flips).
  • Angle-angle (AA): if two angles of one triangle match two angles of another, the triangles are similar. Find the third angle with 180° − a − b.
  • Missing side: find the scale factor from a pair of matching sides, then multiply (or divide).
Triangle 45°, 75° (third = 60°) and triangle 45°, 60° (third = 75°) → similar

5 · Slope

Slope is a number that describes how steep a line is.

slope = vertical change (rise)horizontal change (run)
From (x₁, y₁) to (x₂, y₂): y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁
  • Slope triangles on the same line are always similar, always the same ratio of sides, so a line has one slope.
  • Uphill → positive. Downhill → negative. Flat → 0. Vertical → undefined.
  • Is a point on the line? Find the slope from a point on the line to the new point. Same slope → yes. Different → no.
  • Equation of a line: use a general point (x, y). The slope triangle from (7, 11) to (x, y) is y − 11 tall and x − 7 wide, so y − 11x − 7 = slope. If the line goes through (0, 0): yx = slope.

Vocabulary

Scaled copy
A copy where every length is multiplied by the same scale factor; same shape, same angles.
Scale factor
The number you multiply each length in the original by to get the scaled copy.
Scale
A ratio of drawing distance to actual distance, like 1 inch : 50 feet.
Corresponding parts
Matching sides, angles, or points in the original and the copy (A matches A′).
Dilation
A transformation that changes size but not shape, using a center point and a scale factor.
Center of dilation
The fixed point everything stretches away from or shrinks toward.
Translation
A slide.
Rotation
A turn around a point.
Reflection
A flip across a line.
Similar
A sequence of translations, rotations, reflections, and dilations takes one figure onto the other.
Congruent
Same shape and same size: only translations, rotations, and reflections are needed.
Slope
Vertical change ÷ horizontal change. How steep a line is.
Slope triangle
A right triangle whose long side lies on the line; its legs are the rise and the run.
